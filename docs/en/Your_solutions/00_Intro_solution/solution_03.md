# Physics Analysis: Proportionality & Gravity
**Formula:** $F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$

---

## 1. Objective
To determine the net change in the gravitational force ($F$) when the physical parameters of the system (mass and distance) are modified simultaneously.



---

## 2. Given Changes (New Conditions)
Let the original force be $F_1$. We define the new variables as follows:
* **Distance ($r$):** Doubled $\implies r_{new} = 2r$
* **Mass 1 ($m_1$):** Halved $\implies m_{1,new} = \frac{1}{2}m_1$
* **Mass 2 ($m_2$):** Halved $\implies m_{2,new} = \frac{1}{2}m_2$

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Calculation

### Step A: Set up the New Force Equation ($F_2$)
Substitute the new values into the original formula:
$$F_2 = G \frac{(\frac{1}{2}m_1) (\frac{1}{2}m_2)}{(2r)^2}$$

### Step B: Simplify the Numerator
Multiply the mass fractions:
$$\frac{1}{2}m_1 \times \frac{1}{2}m_2 = \frac{1}{4}m_1 m_2$$

### Step C: Simplify the Denominator
Square the doubled distance:
$$(2r)^2 = 4r^2$$

### Step D: Combine and Compare
$$F_2 = G \frac{\frac{1}{4}m_1 m_2}{4r^2}$$
$$F_2 = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{4} \times \left( G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \right)$$
**$$F_2 = \frac{1}{16} F_1$$**

---

## 4. Conclusion
* **Result:** The gravitational force decreases by a factor of **16**.
* **Final Force:** The new force is **$1/16$** (or $6.25\%$) of the original gravitational attraction.
* **Key Insight:** Increasing distance has a much more significant impact on the force than changing mass because distance is **squared** in the denominator (Inverse Square Law).
