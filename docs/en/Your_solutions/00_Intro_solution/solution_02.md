# Mathematical Analysis: Systems of Equations
**Equations:** 1) $2x + 3y = 12$  
2) $x - y = 1$

---

## 1. Objective
To find the unique values of $x$ and $y$ (the intersection point) that satisfy both linear equations simultaneously.



---

## 2. Solving via Substitution Method

### Step A: Isolate a Variable
From Equation 2, it is easiest to express $x$ in terms of $y$:
$$x - y = 1 \implies \mathbf{x = y + 1}$$

### Step B: Substitute into the First Equation
Replace $x$ in Equation 1 with $(y + 1)$:
$$2(y + 1) + 3y = 12$$

### Step C: Solve for $y$
* Distribute: $2y + 2 + 3y = 12$
* Combine like terms: $5y + 2 = 12$
* Isolate $y$: $5y = 10$
* **$y = 2$**

---

## 3. Finding the Second Variable
Substitute the value of $y = 2$ back into our isolated $x$ equation:
* $x = y + 1$
* $x = 2 + 1$
* **$x = 3$**

---

## 4. Final Verification
Check the values $(3, 2)$ in both original equations:
* **Eq 1:** $2(3) + 3(2) = 6 + 6 = 12$ (Correct!)
* **Eq 2:** $3 - 2 = 1$ (Correct!)

---

## 5. Conclusion
* **Solution Set:** $(x, y) = (3, 2)$
* **Graphical Interpretation:** The two lines intersect at the point **(3, 2)** on a Cartesian plane.
